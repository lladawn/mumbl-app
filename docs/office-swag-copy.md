# Office landing — swag copy

_Copy only, 2026-08-30. Author: Phyllis. For Stanley's landing rebuild — drop lines in, no code here._
_Positioning: this is not a utility pitch. Nobody needs this. They want one. Every line below is written for "cool," not "convinced." Word count is the constraint, not the target._

Grounded in the actual demo (`/office/demo`, walked with WASD) and what's already shipped: apps become stations (VS Code, terminal, Figma, Zoom, Slack, Spotify), a café, a rec room with a ping-pong scoreboard and an arcade cabinet, named people with live status pills, a day recap ("you built something today," per-app time breakdown), and a shareable card. Nothing below claims anything beyond that.

---

## 1. Hero line

**Pick: your apps just got bodies.**

Specific (it's about the actual mechanic — every tool becomes a character), a little strange, says nothing about *why* it's useful because it isn't trying to. See §6 for why this is the recommendation over the others.

### Alternates (different angles, pick one or steal pieces)

| Line | Angle |
|---|---|
| `everyone's getting an office. get yours.` | pure FOMO / scarce object — the swag framing, literally |
| `your life, shown as a game world.` | the founder's own words, stated straight, no spin needed |
| `your day, playable.` | maximum minimalism, leans on mystery to earn the click |

| `your workday finally looks this good.` | glow-up / flex framing — before vs. after |

## 3. Alpha CTA line

**Pick: `get a desk.`**

Alternates: `get in.` / `claim a desk.`

None of these say "download," "sign up," or "install" — it's a private alpha with no signed build and no date, and the line should read like a door, not a form. If a post-submit confirmation line is needed: `you're in. we'll be in touch.`

## 5. What was deliberately left out

No feature claims not already visible in the demo or the shipped page (`app/office/page.jsx`) — no invented stations, no made-up stat, no "AI" anywhere. No line implying you can use this today; every CTA points at the alpha door, not a download. No adjectives doing the work a real detail should do (no "revolutionary," "seamless," "powerful") — per the existing recap voice doc, a real number or a real object (a desk, a scoreboard, a turntable) always beats an adjective.

---

## 6. Recommendation

**Hero: `your apps just got bodies.`**

Reasoning: the brief is explicit that this isn't a utility sell — nobody needs this, they want one, it's swag. That rules out anything hero-line that explains a benefit (`everyone's getting an office` still half-works but leans on borrowed FOMO rather than the product itself; `your day, playable` is cool but abstract enough that it could be literally any game). `your apps just got bodies` is the one line that is *only* describable from having actually seen the thing — it names the exact mechanic that makes someone stop and look (Figma became a person, Spotify became a person), it's stated as a fact that already happened to *your* stuff, and it's weird in a way that reads as confident rather than trying. It also does the least explaining, which is the whole point: if it needs a second line, it's wrong.

**CTA: `get a desk.`** — matches the hero's register (a desk is a specific object in the world you just described, not a generic "waitlist"), and "get" over "join" makes the ask feel like acquiring something scarce, not signing a form.

I'd hold the card headline as-is (`"{name}'s office, right now."`) rather than swap it — it's already doing the swagger-through-understatement thing well and doesn't need a punch-up.
